What is moong dal tadka?

Moong dal tadka is split yellow lentils simmered soft, then finished with a sizzling tempering of ghee, cumin, garlic and tomato. It's the everyday dal of North Indian homes: light, high in protein and on the table in half an hour.

About my recipe

If there's one recipe every working mom should have memorised, it's a fast dal. Yellow moong is the hero here: it cooks in about 20 minutes on the stove (or 3 whistles in a pressure cooker), and it's gentle enough for little ones too.

I take a small bowl out for my toddler before the tadka goes in, then add the chilli for the grown-ups.

Ingredients & substitutes

You'll need 14 ingredients, most of them pantry staples. The full amounts are in the recipe card. If you're missing something, here's what I swap in:

Yellow moong dalRed lentils (masoor) cook just as fast. Toor dal works too, but soak it 30 minutes first.
GheeAny neutral oil or avocado oil keeps it dairy-free.
Garlic and onionSkip both for a Jain-style dal and add an extra pinch of hing.
Fresh tomato1 tbsp tomato paste or 2 tbsp canned crushed tomatoes.

How to make it

  1. 1

    Add the rinsed dal, water, turmeric and salt to a pot. Bring to a boil, skim the foam, then simmer partly covered for 20–25 minutes until soft (or pressure cook for 3 whistles).

  2. 2

    Whisk the cooked dal lightly so it turns creamy. Add a splash of hot water if it's too thick.

  3. 3

    For the tadka, heat ghee in a small pan. Add cumin and hing, and let the cumin sizzle.

  4. 4

    Add garlic and fry until just golden, then the onion until soft. Add tomato and cook for 3–4 minutes until it breaks down.

  5. 5

    Turn off the heat, stir in the red chillies and chilli powder, and pour the tadka over the dal.

  6. 6

    Finish with coriander and lemon. Serve with rice or roti.

Expert tips

  • Rinse the dal until the water runs mostly clear. It cuts the foam and gives a cleaner taste.
  • Pull out a toddler portion before the chilli goes in, then season the rest for the grown-ups.
  • Dal thickens as it sits. Loosen leftovers with hot water, not cold.
  • Add the chilli powder off the heat so it colours the ghee without burning.

FAQs

Do I need to soak moong dal?

No. Split yellow moong cooks in 20–25 minutes on the stove without soaking.

Can I make it in an Instant Pot?

Yes. Pressure cook the dal, water, turmeric and salt for 6 minutes, natural release for 10, then make the tadka on the stove.

How long does it keep?

Up to 3 days in the fridge, or 2 months frozen. Make a fresh tadka when reheating for the best flavour.

30-minute moong dal tadka

The dal I make when it's 6pm, everyone is hungry and I have nothing planned. Yellow moong cooks fast and needs no soaking.

Prep
5 min
Cook
25 min
Total
30 min
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • 1 cup yellow moong dal, rinsed well
  • 3½ cups water
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp salt (adjust)
  • 1 tbsp ghee or oil
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • Pinch of hing (asafoetida)
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tomato, chopped
  • 1–2 dried red chillies (optional)
  • ½ tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
  • Handful of chopped coriander
  • Squeeze of lemon

Method

  1. Add the rinsed dal, water, turmeric and salt to a pot. Bring to a boil, skim the foam, then simmer partly covered for 20–25 minutes until soft (or pressure cook for 3 whistles).
  2. Whisk the cooked dal lightly so it turns creamy. Add a splash of hot water if it's too thick.
  3. For the tadka, heat ghee in a small pan. Add cumin and hing, and let the cumin sizzle.
  4. Add garlic and fry until just golden, then the onion until soft. Add tomato and cook for 3–4 minutes until it breaks down.
  5. Turn off the heat, stir in the red chillies and chilli powder, and pour the tadka over the dal.
  6. Finish with coriander and lemon. Serve with rice or roti.
