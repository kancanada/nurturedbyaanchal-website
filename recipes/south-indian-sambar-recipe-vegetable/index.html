South Indian vegetable sambar

What is sambar?

Sambar is a South Indian stew of toor dal and vegetables cooked in tamarind water with sambar powder, then finished with a tempering of mustard seeds, fenugreek, red chillies and curry leaves. It's served with rice for lunch and with idli, dosa or vada for breakfast.

About my recipe

Sambar is the everyday lentil and vegetable stew of South Indian homes. It looks like a long recipe, but most of the work is the pressure cooker cooking the dal while you chop the vegetables.

A store-bought sambar powder keeps this weeknight-friendly. For little ones, keep the red chillies out of the tempering and serve the sambar mixed with rice and a little ghee.

  2. 2

    Soak the tamarind in ½ cup hot water for 10 minutes, squeeze and strain the juice. Skip this if using tamarind paste.

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

  6. 6

    For the tempering, heat oil in a small pan. Add mustard seeds and let them pop, then fenugreek, red chillies, curry leaves and hing. Pour over the sambar, add coriander and serve.

Expert tips

  • Cook the dal until it is completely soft and mash it well. Smooth dal gives sambar its body.
  • Cook the vegetables in the tamarind water until just tender, not mushy.
  • Add the tempering at the very end so the curry leaves stay fragrant.
  • Sambar thickens as it cools. Loosen it with hot water when reheating.

FAQs

Can I make sambar without a pressure cooker?

Yes. Simmer the rinsed toor dal in a covered pot for 40 to 45 minutes, adding water as needed, until it mashes easily.

What vegetables go in sambar?

Drumstick, pearl onions, pumpkin, carrot, okra, brinjal, radish and potato are all common. Use what you have.

Is sambar OK for kids?

It's usually mild. Leave out the red chillies and serve it mixed with rice and ghee for little ones.

Can I freeze sambar?

Yes, for up to 2 months. Leave out potato if you plan to freeze it, as it can turn grainy.

South Indian vegetable sambar

An easy South Indian sambar recipe: toor dal, vegetables, tamarind and sambar powder with a curry leaf tempering. For rice, idli or dosa.

Prep
10 min
Cook
30 min
Total
40 min
Serves
4

Ingredients

  • ¾ cup toor dal (split pigeon peas), rinsed
  • 2½ cups water for the dal
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • Small lemon-sized ball of tamarind, or 1 tsp tamarind paste
  • 2 cups mixed vegetables (drumstick, carrot, pumpkin, okra, brinjal, pearl onions)
  • 1 tomato, chopped
  • 2 tbsp sambar powder
  • 1¼ tsp salt (adjust)
  • 1 tsp jaggery or sugar (optional)
  • 1½ cups water for the vegetables
  • 1 tbsp oil or ghee
  • 1 tsp mustard seeds
  • ¼ tsp fenugreek seeds (methi)
  • 1 to 2 dried red chillies
  • 10 curry leaves
  • Pinch of hing (asafoetida)
  • 2 tbsp chopped coriander

Method

  1. Pressure cook the toor dal with 2½ cups water and the turmeric for 4 to 5 whistles until completely soft (or simmer for 40 minutes). Mash it until smooth.
  2. Soak the tamarind in ½ cup hot water for 10 minutes, squeeze and strain the juice. Skip this if using tamarind paste.
  3. In a pot, cook the vegetables and tomato with 1½ cups water and the salt for 8 to 10 minutes until just tender.
  4. Add the tamarind water and sambar powder and simmer for 5 minutes, until the raw smell of the tamarind goes.
  5. Stir in the mashed dal and jaggery. Add hot water if it is too thick. Simmer for 5 minutes.
  6. For the tempering, heat oil in a small pan. Add mustard seeds and let them pop, then fenugreek, red chillies, curry leaves and hing. Pour over the sambar, add coriander and serve.
