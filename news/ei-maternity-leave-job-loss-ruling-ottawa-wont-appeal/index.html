Ottawa won't appeal EI ruling for moms laid off on leave

A court found EI rules discriminate against moms who lose their job around maternity leave. Ottawa now has until September 2027 to fix them.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

The federal government has confirmed it will not take a major Employment Insurance case to the Supreme Court of Canada. The case was brought by mothers who lost their jobs around the time of their maternity or parental leave and were then denied regular EI benefits. The decision means a Federal Court of Appeal ruling in their favour now stands, and Ottawa has to change the law.

What the problem was

Under the current EI rules, a parent who is laid off shortly before, during or soon after maternity or parental leave can end up with little or no regular EI, the benefit most people rely on after a job loss. The time spent on leave can leave her short of the insurable hours she needs, or she runs into the limit on how many weeks of combined benefits can be paid in one benefit period. In plain terms, taking leave to have a baby could cost a mother the safety net she'd have had if she had never gone on leave.

According to Canadian Lawyer, the rules at issue include the 52-week qualifying and benefit periods, the cap on combined regular and special benefits, and the fact that the qualifying period can be extended for things like illness, but not for time spent on maternity or parental benefits.

How the case got here

The case was started in 2018 by six Quebec women whose job losses happened between 2016 and 2018, with support from the advocacy group Mouvement Action-Chômage de Montréal, Global News reported. The Social Security Tribunal first ruled for them in 2022. That decision was overturned on appeal within the tribunal in 2024, and the women then took it to the Federal Court of Appeal.

In early September 2026, a three-judge panel of the Federal Court of Appeal ruled unanimously for the women. The court found the rules violate the equality guarantee in section 15 of the Charter of Rights and Freedoms, because they discriminate on the basis of sex. As the court put it, reported by The Canadian Press, "only women can become pregnant and give birth."

Justice Gleason, writing for the court, said more generous maternity and parental benefits "cannot be the forfeiture of regular EI benefits", according to Canadian Lawyer. The court declared the provisions inoperative but suspended that declaration for one year to give the government time to respond. The six women's claims were sent back to the EI Commission to be looked at again.

What happens now

On November 10, the office of federal minister Patty Hajdu, who is responsible for EI, confirmed the government would not appeal, CTV News reported, citing The Canadian Press. The government said it would work on solutions over the coming year. La Presse Canadienne reported that the deadline to change the law is September 3, 2027.

As of today, no bill or regulation has been announced, so the EI rules themselves have not changed yet. The case is about the federal Employment Insurance Act, which applies to workers across Canada, including here in B.C. Mouvement Action-Chômage has said thousands of women could benefit; there is no official count.

If you were laid off around a maternity or parental leave and were refused regular EI, it's worth keeping your paperwork, your Record of Employment and any decision letters. Service Canada, a legal clinic or an employment lawyer can tell you what applies to your own situation.

What I think

I went back to my full-time job after my son was born, and I know how much planning goes into a leave. The idea that losing your job during it could also take away your EI is exactly the kind of motherhood penalty nobody warns you about. Six moms spent eight years fighting this for the rest of us. Now I want to see the fix actually written into the law before the deadline, not at the last minute.
