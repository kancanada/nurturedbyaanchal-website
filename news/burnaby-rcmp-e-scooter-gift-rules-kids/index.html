Thinking of an e-scooter gift? B.C.'s age rule is 16

Burnaby RCMP wrote a letter to Santa about e-scooters. The rules parents should know before buying one for a child or teen this Christmas.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 27, 2026 · 4 min read

If an e-scooter is on your child's Christmas list, Burnaby RCMP would like a word, with Santa.

On November 26 the detachment published a news release and video in the form of a letter to Santa Claus from its media relations officer, Cpl. Mike Kalanj. The message behind the holiday humour is serious: a lot of kids are asking for e-scooters this year, and many of them aren't legally allowed to ride one.

The rules in the letter

The letter tells Santa that in Burnaby you must be at least 16 to ride an e-scooter, and that the same age rule applies in every B.C. municipality that's part of the province's Electric Kick Scooter Pilot Project. It also reminds him that many B.C. municipalities are not in the project at all, and that if he does give an e-scooter to someone old enough, he should include a bicycle helmet, because it's the law.

Cpl. Kalanj wrote that police don't want a child to "receive a gift they aren't legally allowed to use," Burnaby's Freshet News reported. The RCMP also reminded families that e-dirt bikes are not street legal, and that e-bikes have their own separate rules.

Where e-scooters are allowed in Metro Vancouver

E-scooters can only be ridden on public roads in communities that have joined the pilot. According to the Province of B.C., participating communities in Greater Vancouver and the Fraser Valley currently include Burnaby, Coquitlam, Port Moody, Richmond, Vancouver, the City and District of North Vancouver, West Vancouver, Bowen Island, the Township of Langley, Squamish and Chilliwack.

If your city isn't on that list, an e-scooter can't legally be ridden on public roads there, whatever the rider's age. Check the province's page before you buy, because communities can join over time.

What a legal e-scooter looks like

The province also sets rules for the scooter itself. Among them: it can't have a seat, its motor output can't be more than 500 watts, it can't weigh more than 45 kg, and its top speed can't be more than 25 km/h on a flat, paved surface. It needs brakes that can stop it within 7.5 metres from top speed, and a bell or horn. Riders don't need a licence or insurance, but they can be ticketed for riding a device that doesn't meet the rules, so it's worth checking the specs before buying online.

Why police are raising it now

E-scooters, e-bikes and e-dirt bikes have become popular gifts, and many are sold online with little information about local rules. A scooter that is legal to sell in a store isn't necessarily legal to ride on the street in your city, or for your child. The province's rules also say riders must follow any extra rules their own municipality sets, so the details can change from one city to the next.

Burnaby RCMP pointed families to the City of Burnaby's own e-scooter and e-bike page for local rules. Other cities in the pilot, including Coquitlam, publish their own pages too.

Before you buy

• Check the rider's age. Under 16 means no e-scooter on public roads in B.C.
• Check whether your city is part of the pilot.
• Check the scooter's specs against the provincial rules, especially speed, power and seat.
• Budget for a proper bicycle helmet.
• Look up your own city's rules too, since participating communities can add their own.

What I think

I love that the RCMP made this a letter to Santa, because it's exactly the kind of gift that sounds fun in a store and turns into a problem on Boxing Day. My son is two, so this is a few years away for us, but those years come fast. If your kid really wants wheels this year, a regular scooter or a bike with a good helmet might be the gift that actually gets used.
