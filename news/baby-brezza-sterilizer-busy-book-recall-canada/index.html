Recalled in Canada: Baby Brezza sterilizer and a busy book

Health Canada recalls a Baby Brezza bottle sterilizer for overheating and a toddler busy book for choking risk, plus a warning on a banned baby walker.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

With holiday shopping starting and a lot of baby gear changing hands, here's a round-up of recent Health Canada recalls and warnings that affect babies and toddlers. If you have any of these at home, check the model numbers below.

Baby Brezza SuperFast Sterilizer Dryer

Health Canada has recalled the Baby Brezza SuperFast Sterilizer Dryer, a countertop machine that sterilizes and dries bottles and pump parts. The affected model is BRZ0083 (UPC 886267008313).

The problem is overheating. Health Canada says the unit "can overheat when plugged in and in use", which creates fire and burn hazards, Global News reported. As of October 29, there had been 20 reports of overheating and melting in Canada and 32 in the U.S. No injuries have been reported.

About 6,001 units were sold in Canada and 29,000 in the U.S., between September 2023 and November 2026. Health Canada says to stop using it right away. The Quebec-based importer, EI Brand Management Inc., is offering a free replacement through service@elfe.ca or the brand's recall page at babybrezza.ca/en/recalls/brz0083, CP24 reported.

The sterilizer was announced alongside other fire and burn recalls in early November, including Bose S1 Pro and S1 Pro+ battery packs for portable speakers and NEWDERY power banks (model ZHX-PB22) sold on Amazon.ca. If you have a portable speaker or a power bank in the diaper bag, those are worth a quick check too.

Esjay Montessori busy book (blue dinosaur)

A toddler activity book sold on Amazon.ca, the blue dinosaur Esjay Montessori busy book, was recalled on October 7 because small red pieces on page 2 can come loose. That's a choking hazard for children under three, MTL Blog reported.

This one sold widely: 32,340 units in Canada between August 2023 and September 2026. No incidents or injuries had been reported in Canada as of September 22. The fix is simple: unzip and remove the page with the red pieces, write "RECALLED" on both sides, throw it out, and email a photo to esjay-service@outlook.com for a free replacement page.

Warning: MUELODSIC baby walker

Health Canada has also warned against a MUELODSIC multifunctional baby walker that was sold on Amazon.ca (Amazon ID B0FWGCCJ14). Baby walkers are banned in Canada because of the risk of falls, including down stairs. Amazon reported 133 units sold in Canada. Because this is a warning and not a recall, there may be no refund. Health Canada's advice is to stop using it and destroy it.

Recall or warning: what's the difference?

A recall means the company is working with Health Canada, so there's usually a refund, repair or replacement. A warning is issued when the seller isn't cooperating, which often happens with products from third-party sellers on online marketplaces. With a warning, you usually can't count on getting your money back, as MTL Blog explained. That's one more reason to check the brand and seller carefully when you buy baby gear online.

What to do if you have one

Stop using the product, check the model or ID number, and follow the steps above. It is illegal to sell or give away a recalled product in Canada, so please don't pass these on through a buy-and-sell group or a hand-me-down bag. You can search every recall and sign up for alerts on the Government of Canada recalls site.

What I think

Bottle sterilizers and busy books are on so many baby registries, and a lot of us buy them from Amazon or second-hand without ever checking for recalls. If you're buying gifts for a new baby this season or getting gear from a friend, take two minutes and search the recalls site first. And please share this with the mom group chat. That's how most of us find out.
