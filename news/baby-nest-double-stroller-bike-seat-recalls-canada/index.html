Before you pass it on: baby nest, stroller and bike seat recalls

Health Canada's recent baby and toddler recalls: Simmons baby nests, a Valco double stroller, a Hamax bike seat and banned walkers sold on AliExpress.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 26, 2026 · 4 min read

This is the time of year when a lot of baby gear changes hands. Older kids outgrow things, cousins pass down strollers, and buy-and-sell groups fill up before the holidays. Before anything goes into the hand-me-down bag, here are the baby and toddler products Health Canada has recalled or warned about over the past few weeks. Every item below links to the official notice.

Simmons Kozy and Nido baby nests and loungers

On October 22, Health Canada announced a recall of Simmons Kozy and Nido baby nests and loungers. It determined they don't meet Canada's Cribs, Cradles and Bassinets Regulations, which creates a potential fall hazard, according to the recall notice.

The affected models are Kozy 59900 (grey) and 59902 (taupe), and Nido 59950 (ivory), 59955 (taupe), 59960 (coconut milk) and 59965 (moonbeam). The model number is on the warning label on the product. As of October 19, the company had no reports of incidents or injuries in Canada.

Health Canada says to stop using these right away and dispose of them so they can't be used again. Questions go to Jupiter Industries at 1-800-465-5795 or customerservice@jupiterindustries.com. The notice also repeats its reminder that the safest place for a baby to sleep is on their back, on a firm, flat surface meant for sleep.

Valco Baby Trend Duo Double Stroller

This one is unusual: Health Canada ordered the recall. In a notice updated October 1, it said Junior Brands Corp. must recall the Valco Baby Trend Duo Double Stroller after Health Canada's own sampling and testing found it doesn't meet the Carriages and Strollers Regulations, according to the order notice.

Health Canada says the stroller can tip over in use, the leg opening in front of the seat can let a child's torso slip through, which is an entrapment risk, and some areas around the seat can pinch. The affected colours are Night (N0001), Cappuccino (N0048), Cloud (N0460), Denim (N9872) and Charcoal (N9939). As of September 25, there were no reports of incidents or injuries in Canada. Stop using it and contact Junior Brands Corp. at support@juniorbrands.ca.

Hamax Buddy child bike seat

If your toddler rides on the back of your bike, check this one. The Hamax Buddy child bicycle seat, reference numbers 4091054 and 8591602, was recalled on October 26 because the textile label on the harness strap can come off and become a choking hazard, the recall notice says. CTV News also reported the recall.

The good news: you don't have to throw the seat away. Health Canada says to cut off and pull out the label completely, and then the seat can be used as normal. Décathlon Canada is the contact, at 1-844-533-3322 or onlineservice@decathlon.ca. No incidents or injuries had been reported in Canada as of October 20.

Warning: baby walkers sold on AliExpress

On November 5, Health Canada expanded an earlier warning about baby walkers that were sold on AliExpress, adding two more listings: an "oversized heightened" kids walking walker sold as a gait training aid, and a "learning walker" for toddlers, per the alert.

Baby walkers are banned in Canada, because children in them can reach hazards they otherwise couldn't and can fall down stairs. Because this is a warning about online listings and not a company recall, there may be no refund. Health Canada's advice is to stop using the walker and destroy it so nobody else can use it.

Before you sell, gift or pass anything on

It's illegal in Canada to sell, give away or redistribute a recalled product, and that includes Facebook Marketplace and hand-me-downs between friends. Before passing on or accepting baby gear, look for the model number and search the Government of Canada recalls site. You can also report a problem with any product using Health Canada's consumer incident report form.

What I think

Baby nests and loungers are everywhere on social media, and I understand why they're tempting when you need your hands back for ten minutes. But this recall is a good reminder that "sold in a store" doesn't always mean "meets the rules". Before you pass on anything your little one has outgrown, or accept something from a friend, take two minutes on the recalls site. It's worth it.
