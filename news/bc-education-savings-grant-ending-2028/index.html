B.C.'s free $1,200 RESP grant is ending in 2028

B.C. is ending its $1,200 Training and Education Savings Grant in April 2028. Who can still get it, how to apply, and why families want more time.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 27, 2026 · 4 min read

If you have young kids in B.C., there's a deadline worth knowing about. The B.C. Training and Education Savings Grant, better known as the BCTESG, is being shut down. It's the $1,200 the province puts into a child's RESP, with no contribution required from the family.

What's changing

The province's Budget 2026 fiscal plan, released in February, says the grant will be discontinued effective April 1, 2028.

This week The Globe and Mail reported that, as the end date gets closer, some B.C. residents are asking the province to extend the program. As of today, the end date has not changed.

How the grant works today

According to the Province of B.C., the government contributes $1,200 to eligible children. A child can apply between their 6th birthday and the day before they turn 9. The parent or guardian and the child must live in B.C. when applying and need valid Social Insurance Numbers. The federal application form for the grant says it is for children born in 2006 or later.

You apply through a bank or other financial institution that offers RESPs and takes part in the program. The province says it has 56 partners, including most major banks. You don't have to put any of your own money in to get the grant, and you can shop around for an RESP provider that doesn't require a contribution to open the account. If your child already has an RESP with a provider that doesn't offer the grant, the province says you can open a second RESP with one that does.

Who could miss out

Because children can only apply from age 6, the end date matters a lot for younger kids. If the program ends on April 1, 2028 as planned, a child who hasn't turned 6 by then won't be old enough to apply under the current rules. That would include most of today's babies, toddlers and preschoolers.

If your child is between 6 and 8 right now, the most important thing is simple: apply before the grant ends, and before your child's 9th birthday, whichever comes first. Check with your bank about how long the application takes to process.

Why the grant mattered

The BCTESG was designed so that families who couldn't afford to save could still start an RESP. The province's own page says it requires no matching or extra contributions, and encourages families to open an RESP even if they can only put in a small amount, because even small savings can grow over time. Other family members or family friends can also open an RESP for a child if they have the information needed to set it up.

For a family starting with nothing in an RESP, a one-time $1,200 deposit at age 6 gives a child's education savings a head start of more than a decade before post-secondary.

What to do now

• If your child is 6, 7 or 8, ask your bank or RESP provider about the BCTESG this month.
• If your child already has an RESP, check whether the $1,200 has actually been paid in. Your annual RESP statement should show it.
• If your child is younger, open an RESP anyway if it suits your budget. The federal Canada Education Savings Grant and Canada Learning Bond are separate federal programs and are not part of this change.
• Keep an eye on the news. If the province changes the end date, we'll update this story.

What I think

My son is two, so under the current rules he'll never be old enough to apply before this ends. $1,200 is real money when you're starting an RESP from zero. If you have a child aged 6 to 8, please don't leave it sitting there. And if you have friends with kids that age, send them this.
