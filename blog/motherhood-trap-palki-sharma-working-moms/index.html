The motherhood trap: a working mom on why there's no "right" choice

Palki Sharma told three stories of women judged for their motherhood choices. I've lived a version of every one of them.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 24, 2026 · 5 min read

Palki Sharma did a short segment on her show about something every mom I know has felt and almost nobody says out loud: whatever you choose, someone will judge it.

"The Motherhood Trap Women Can't Escape", The Palki Sharma Show on India Global Review (YouTube).

What she said

She told three stories. A senior White House spokesperson stepped back from her job after having a baby, and was criticised for putting family first. A US congresswoman spoke openly about freezing her eggs, and was criticised for putting her career first. A young mayor in Japan announced maternity leave, and the country argued about whether she was allowed to take it.

Her point: three different choices, the same backlash. She also shared a global figure: in 2020, far more mothers of young children than fathers were out of paid work. She called it the motherhood penalty.

Why it hit me

I went back to my full-time job after my son was born. I've heard both versions. "How do you leave him all day?" And, from the other side, "you're so lucky you have time to work out." Nobody says these things to dads.

And I see my friends who chose to stay home get the opposite: "what do you even do all day?" Being a homemaker is one of the toughest, most underrated jobs there is. The judgement just changes shape.

There is no option that society doesn't have a comment on.

Where I'd push it further

Palki ends by saying workplaces should stop making motherhood the price of admission. I agree. But I'd add home to that list. A flexible job means very little if, when you get home, the whole second shift is still yours.

What helps, in my experience, is boring and practical: a partner who doesn't need to be asked, a manager who doesn't blink at a daycare call, and the confidence to stop explaining yourself to everyone else.
