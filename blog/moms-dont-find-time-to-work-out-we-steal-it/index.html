Moms don't find time to work out. We steal it.

"You're so lucky you have time to go to the gym." What that one hour actually costs a working mom, and why I still go.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 25, 2026 · 6 min read

There's one comment I hear more than almost any other: "You're so lucky you have time to go work out as a mom."

I know it's usually meant kindly. But I made a Reel about it, because the word "lucky" hides everything that happens around that one hour. Here's the longer version.

Watch the Reel on Instagram

It's not extra time

Nobody handed me a spare hour. I work full time in home insurance. I have a two-year-old. The day already has more in it than it can hold.

So when I go to the gym, the time doesn't come from nowhere. It comes from somewhere else. The second half of my Reel is me after the workout, basically sprinting through the rest of the day to catch up on everything I put off so I could go. That's the honest picture. Moms don't find time to work out. We steal it, and we pay for it later.

What "paying for it later" looks like

It can be the laundry that waits until late at night. Emails answered faster than you'd like. A dinner that becomes a quick dal instead of something fancy. A shower in record time. Everything that got pushed, now squeezed into the hours that are left.

It's also the planning that nobody sees. Checking who's doing bedtime. Packing the gym bag the night before. Making sure the day still works for everyone else in the house before it works for me.

None of this is a complaint. It's just the part that doesn't fit in a gym selfie.

So why do I still go?

Because after my son was born, I wanted my health and my time back. So I started training again around my workday, and that's a big part of why I started sharing my life on Instagram as Nurtured by Aanchal.

I'm not a trainer and this isn't advice about what anyone else should do with their body. What I can say is that, for me, that hour is worth the scramble afterwards. It's time that belongs to me in a day that mostly belongs to everyone else.

Some weeks I train, some weeks I just survive, and both are fine. A missed week is not a failure, it's a busy week.

Strict schedule or squeeze-it-in?

In my Reel I asked whether you're a strict-schedule mom or a squeeze-it-in-whenever mom. I asked because I don't think there's one right way.

The strict-schedule moms protect a fixed slot, early morning or right after work, and treat it like a meeting they can't cancel. The squeeze-it-in moms grab whatever window opens up: a lunch break, a weekend nap time, 20 minutes at home with a dumbbell while dinner simmers.

What works changes with the season of life. Right now, for me, it's whatever fits around work and a toddler. The only rule I try to keep is that something is better than nothing, and that I don't have to earn it by being "done" with everything else first. I will never be done with everything else.

If you live with a mom who wants that hour

A lot of the time, whether a mom gets to work out has very little to do with motivation and everything to do with who else is holding the fort. If you're her partner, her parent or her in-law, the most useful thing you can do is make the hour possible without making her ask for it every single time.

Take bedtime on her gym night. Do the dinner dishes. Handle the morning so she can go early. And when she comes back, don't hand her the list of everything that happened while she was gone. That's how the hour turns into a debt instead of a break.

Starting small counts

If going to a gym feels impossible right now, that's completely okay. Movement doesn't have to look like my Reel. A walk with the stroller, a stretch after the kids are down, a short home workout during nap time: it all counts, and it all starts the habit of putting yourself somewhere on the list.

What I'd love people to say instead

If you see a mom who makes it to the gym, or the yoga class, or the walk around the block, maybe skip "you're so lucky". Try "good for you" or "how do you fit it in?" And then actually listen to the answer, because the answer usually involves a partner who steps up, a grandparent who helps, a very early alarm, or a lot of things quietly moved around.

And if you're a mom who wants to start but feels guilty taking the time: the guilt is normal, and it doesn't mean you're doing something wrong. Start small. Your family will be fine for one hour. You might be surprised how much better you are for them when you come back.

If you're newly postpartum, please check with your doctor before starting or changing any exercise. Every body and every recovery is different.
