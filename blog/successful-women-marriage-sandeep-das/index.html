"Nobody likes a successful woman"? A working wife responds

Sandeep Das told Raj Shamani that successful, independent women struggle to marry because of how we're 'wired'. Here's where I agree and where I don't.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Sandeep Das is back in my feed, this time with another big claim on Raj Shamani's podcast: that in Indian society, nobody really likes a successful, independent woman, and that's why so many high-achieving women struggle to find a partner.

You can find the conversation by searching "Sandeep Das Raj Shamani successful women" on YouTube.

What he argued

His reasoning has two parts. First, he says some men feel a very independent woman doesn't need them and can't be "controlled", and some older women resent younger women living life on their own terms when they never could.

Second, he argues that men and women are wired differently: men look for beauty and peace, women look for resources, so a woman earning well wants someone earning more, and that man may prefer a less independent partner. He shares a classroom experiment and says this pattern has existed for thousands of years and won't change.

Where he has a point

The first part is real. I've seen strong, self-made women judged for drinking once at a party, for buying their own flat, for being "too much". The comments rarely come from strangers. They come from aunties, relatives, sometimes other women.

Where I disagree

I don't accept that this is fixed wiring that will run for another thousand years. Preferences are shaped by what we grow up seeing. My son is growing up watching his mom go to work every day, and that will shape what normal looks like to him.

And "men want peace" shouldn't mean "men want a woman with no opinions". Peace in a marriage comes from respect and sharing the load, not from one person shrinking.

Empowerment isn't about competing with men. It's about refusing to feel small.

To the successful women reading this: you don't need to dim yourself to be loveable. The right partner is proud of your salary, not threatened by it.

Is he right, or is this changing?

Have you seen this play out, as a woman or a man? Tell me on Instagram or email me. I'd love to hear especially from men who are genuinely happy their partner earns more.
