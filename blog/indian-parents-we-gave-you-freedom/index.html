"We gave you freedom": are Indian parents toxic, or just scared?

A psychiatrist on Raj Shamani's podcast explained why Indian parents interfere so much. My Short on this got 60K views, so let's go deeper.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 24, 2026 · 6 min read

My YouTube Short asking whether Indian parents are toxic, or whether it's unintentional emotional guilt, crossed 60K views. Clearly a lot of you feel this. So when I found this clip, I had to write about it.

"Love, Control or Fear? Why Indian Parents Interfere", a psychiatrist on Raj Shamani Clips (YouTube).

What the psychiatrist said

He starts with a line every Indian kid has heard: "we gave you so much freedom." His point is that freedom isn't something parents hand out. It's already yours.

Then he explains where the control comes from. Our parents' generation worked incredibly hard, often with very little money and no safety net. Their way of living kept the family safe, so anything different feels wrong to them, not because it is wrong, but because it's unfamiliar. A lot of it is fear: of society, of money, of their child getting hurt.

He's also honest that emotional guilt is very strong in Indian families, and that trying to completely change a 60-year-old's belief system is close to impossible. His practical advice: decide what you actually want, accept that there may be consequences, and stop spending all your energy trying to convert your parents.

Where I land

This is exactly what I was getting at in my Short. Most of our parents aren't villains. They're scared, and fear comes out as control. That doesn't make the guilt okay, but it makes it understandable.

Now that I'm a parent, I see it from both sides. I already catch myself wanting to protect my son from every hard thing. The question I keep asking myself is: is this about his safety, or about my comfort?

You can love your parents and still make your own choices.

And on log kya kahenge: it's the most expensive excuse we use to hold ourselves, and especially women, back. The neighbours won't pay your bills or live with your regrets.
