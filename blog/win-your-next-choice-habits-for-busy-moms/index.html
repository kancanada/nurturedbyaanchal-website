Forget changing your life. Just win your next choice.

Three of my Reels say the same thing in different ways: stop waiting for a big reset, believe you deserve more, and keep small promises to yourself.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Every January, and honestly every Monday, a lot of us promise ourselves a whole new life. New routine, new body, new habits, new everything. And by Thursday, most of it is gone.

I've been posting a few short Reels about this lately, and when I put them side by side I realised they're all saying the same thing. So here's the longer version, in one place.

Watch the Reel on Instagram

Win your next choice

The first one says: forget "changing your life". Just win your next choice.

I love this idea because it's the only version of change that fits into a mom's day. I work full time in home insurance and I'm raising a two-year-old. I don't have a spare week to reinvent myself. What I do have is the next hour, the next meal and the next thought.

Your body, your focus and your mindset aren't built by the promises you make about someday. They're built by what you do on repeat. That's good news, because it means you don't need a perfect plan. You need the next small choice to go a little better than the last one.

What does that look like in real life? It can be as small as:

• Going to bed twenty minutes earlier instead of scrolling.
• Taking a ten-minute walk at lunch instead of eating at your desk.
• Packing tomorrow's lunch while dinner is cooking.
• Putting the phone in another room for the first half hour with your child after work.
• Saying one kind thing to yourself when you'd normally say something harsh.

None of these will change your life on their own. Repeated for a few months, they quietly do.

You don't need a new life. You need a better next hour.

Choose your hard

Another Reel I posted had one line on it: choose your suffering, the pain of discipline today or the pain of regret tomorrow.

That sounds dramatic, but it's really just honest. Everything has a cost. Getting up early for a workout is hard. Not doing it and feeling stuck six months later is also hard. Saying no to something you don't have the energy for is uncomfortable. Saying yes and resenting it is uncomfortable too.

There's no easy option, only a choice about which kind of hard you'd rather carry. You're not choosing between hard and easy. You're choosing between two kinds of hard, and one of them leaves you better off.

To be clear, this isn't about pushing through when your body or your mind is telling you to stop. Rest is a choice too, and some weeks it's the right one. A missed week is not a failure.

Believe you deserve it

The third Reel is the one that got the most views of the three. The words on screen say you really can have anything you want in this life if you have the audacity to believe you deserve it. In the caption I wrote: your life only expands to the size of what you believe you're worthy of.

I think this is the piece that sits under the other two. A lot of women, and especially a lot of moms, don't fail at habits because they're lazy. They stop because, deep down, they don't think they're allowed to want more. More time. More money. More energy. More of a life that's their own.

We're raised to put everyone else first, and then we become mothers and that instinct goes into overdrive. Wanting something for yourself starts to feel selfish. So you wait for permission that never comes.

Stop asking for permission to want more. That doesn't mean ignoring your family. It means believing you are also part of the family whose needs count.

Keep the promises you make to yourself

My caption ended with this: build the habits, keep the promises you make to yourself, and watch everything shift.

We are so good at keeping promises to other people. We'd never stand up a friend or miss a work deadline without a reason. But the promise to ourselves, the workout, the early night, the hour to read, is the first thing we cancel when the day gets busy.

Every time you keep a small promise to yourself, you're proving that you can be trusted. Every time you break one, you're teaching yourself that your plans don't really count. That's why I'd rather make a tiny promise and keep it than a big one I'll drop by Wednesday.

So make the promise small enough that you can keep it on your worst day. Ten minutes, not an hour. One glass of water before coffee, not a whole new diet. Then keep it. Then make the next one.

A simple way to start this week

If you want to try this, here's what I'd suggest. Pick one area: your body, your money, your mornings, your mind. Choose one small daily action in that area that takes under fifteen minutes. Write it down somewhere you'll see it. And each night, just mark whether you did it.

That's it. No big reset. No perfect Monday. Just a lot of next choices, one after another, made by a woman who believes she's worth the effort.

What's the one small promise you're going to keep this week?
