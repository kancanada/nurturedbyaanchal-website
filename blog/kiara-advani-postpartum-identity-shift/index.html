Kiara Advani said nobody talks about postpartum. She's right.

On Raj Shamani's podcast, Kiara Advani spoke about the identity shift after having a baby and giving yourself grace. A working mom's take.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 24, 2026 · 5 min read

Kiara Advani talking about life six months after having her daughter has stayed with me all week.

"Kiara Advani on the Emotional Reality of Motherhood", Raj Shamani Clips (YouTube).

What she shared

She said she feels like a completely different person since becoming a mom, and that postpartum is something people don't talk about enough. It shows up differently for every woman, emotionally and physically.

She spoke about the identity shift, about being harsh on herself in the early months, and about having to teach herself to give herself grace. She also said something I loved: that mothers and homemakers do more work than most of us can imagine, even though people assume they're "not doing anything" at home.

Why it landed for me

Becoming a mom changed everything, including how much of me was left at the end of the day. Nobody prepares you for the part where you don't quite recognise yourself.

That's a big reason I started sharing my postpartum fitness journey. Not to show a perfect "bounce back", but to show the slow, real version: some weeks I train, some weeks I just survive, and both are fine.

Grace isn't a reward for getting it right. It's what lets you keep going.

What I'd tell a new mom

Your identity didn't disappear. It's being rebuilt. Talk about it, with your partner, your friends, your doctor if something feels off. And stop measuring yourself against a version of you that existed before the baby.
