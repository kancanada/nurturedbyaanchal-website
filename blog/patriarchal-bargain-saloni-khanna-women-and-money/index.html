The "patriarchal bargain": why women keep the cycle going (and how to break it)

On Raj Shamani's podcast, Saloni Khanna explained the patriarchal bargain and why so many women still hand over money decisions. A mom who works in insurance reacts.

By Aanchal Bathija · November 24, 2026 · 6 min read

This episode is older, but one idea from it has stuck with me for days: the patriarchal bargain.

"Cheating Culture, Modern Dating, Patriarchy & Feminism", Saloni Khanna on Figuring Out with Raj Shamani (YouTube). The patriarchy section is near the end.

What she explained

Saloni Khanna, an economist and educator, describes it as a concept from sociology. A woman who suffered under patriarchy as a young daughter-in-law later becomes the mother-in-law, and instead of ending the cycle, she passes it on. "We went through worse, so you can handle this." She gets out of the prison, and lets the next woman in.

She also gives an example many of us know: a woman tells her mother-in-law she's being verbally abused, and is told she's lucky it isn't worse.

Before that, she and Raj discuss women and money. Many women still hand their salary or financial decisions to someone else. Raj pushes back that financial literacy is a problem for everyone in India, men included, and they land somewhere in the middle: it comes down to who around you is talking about money.

Why this one is personal

I work in insurance. I can tell you that the conversations Saloni describes, about which policy to buy, what to do if something happens, where the money actually is, are exactly the ones women are too often left out of. Not because we can't understand them, but because nobody brings us into the room.

Financial independence isn't about comparison. It's about freedom.

And the patriarchal bargain explains so much of log kya kahenge. A lot of the pressure on young wives doesn't come from husbands. It comes from women who were hurt and never got to heal.

How we break it

Start small. Know your own accounts and policies. Talk about money with your girlfriends, not just recipes and kids. And if you're the elder in the house one day, decide that what you went through stops with you.
